## Spot-check — 50 precision + 50 recall

Sampled with `scripts/abs71_spot_check_sample.py` (seed `71`, so the
sample is reproducible). Methodology:

- **Precision (n=50)** — stratified by tagged attribute with a floor
  of one fragment per attribute, then the remaining quota weighted by
  tag frequency. Tests "of the tags we wrote, are they right?"
- **Recall (n=50)** — random across all parsed fragments, independent
  of tag state. Tests "of the clauses that should have a tag, did we
  write one?"

### Precision findings: 47 / 50 = **94%** ✅ (target ≥ 90%)

Three soft calls in the no-column, every other tag judged correct
against the taxonomy definition.

| # | fragment | sampled_for | verdict | note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3 | 6870 | `building_height_storeys` | weak | clause regulates a setback above the streetwall height; tag rests on "implies storey-based" reasoning, not a direct height statement |
| 5 | 6205 | `flood_overlay` | weak | clause is about an engineer's certification of mitigation measures; tag attaches via parent flood/coastal context, not the clause text |
| 14 | 9427 | `occupancy_type` | weak | "Mobile Home Use means…" is a *use-class definition*, not an occupancy-type regulation in the building-code sense |

Even the soft calls are *not wrong-direction* tags — they're tags the
evaluator would have to think about, not tags pointing at the wrong
attribute. Strong-correct rate: 47/50 = 94%. The strongest signal in
the sample is the rationale style: every accepted proposal cites a
specific phrase in the clause, no hedging. The hedge filter is doing
its job (14 hedge discards in the full run).

### Recall findings: depends on the definition of "should be tagged"

The recall sample picked up an inherent ambiguity in the spec: should
**definitional** clauses ("Retail Use means…") be tagged with the
attribute they define, or only **regulatory** clauses ("retail uses
permitted in CEN-1 zone")?

The LLM's behaviour is consistently regulation-only. Rationales talk
about what a clause *regulates* or *applies to*, never about what it
*defines*. That's actually the right call for the evaluator: when
someone submits `primary_use_class = retail`, the evaluator wants to
match a clause that says "retail is permitted here with these
constraints", not a clause that just defines what "retail" means.

Two views on recall, against the same 50-fragment sample:

| view | TP | FN | recall | meets ≥80%? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Regulation-only** (definitions are not misses) | 12 | 3 | **80%** | ✅ exactly at target |
| **Inclusive** (definitions count as misses) | 12 | 9 | **57%** | ❌ |

The three regulation-only false negatives:

| # | fragment | clause | what we'd expect tagged | why missed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8 | 7958 | "rear addition to a Schmidtville Heritage Building" | also `rear_setback_m` | tagged `heritage_overlay` only; the LLM treated "rear addition" as a heritage concept, not a setback concept |
| 41 | 8251 | "it is required to reinstate or maintain character-defining elements" (inside a heritage painting parent) | `heritage_overlay` (via inheritance) | the prefilter caught the parent context, but the LLM declined to tag the child clause |
| 44 | 8245 | "streetwall height for any new main building… within 2.0 metres of the cornice line height established by the registered heritage building" | also `building_height_m` | tagged `heritage_overlay` only |

All three are mis-prioritisations rather than blind spots: the
prefilter routed them to the LLM, and the LLM picked one tag instead
of two. The retrieval-time fix is fine here — these clauses *will*
surface for the wrongly-untagged attribute via cross-keyword retrieval
as a fallback; they just won't surface via the fast tag filter.

### Recall caveat — definitional misses

Six of nine inclusive-view false negatives are taxonomy-definition
clauses ("Mobile Home Use means…", "Boating Club Use means…", etc.).
Whether these *ought* to be tagged is a spec call, not an LLM-quality
call. Two options:

1. **Status quo (regulation-only).** Definitions don't get tagged.
   This is what the LLM is doing today, and it matches the
   evaluator's needs: when "primary_use_class = retail" is submitted,
   matching the "Retail Use means…" definition adds no signal.
2. **Tag definitions too.** Useful only if a downstream agent needs
   to enumerate "tell me every clause that mentions retail", which
   today's evaluator doesn't.

I'd leave the LLM alone unless ABS-42 explicitly wants option (2);
the alternative is a noisier tag index without a consumer.

### Verdict

- **Precision 94%** — clears the ABS-42 ≥ 90% bar.
- **Recall 80% (regulation-only) / 57% (inclusive)** — meets the
  ≥ 80% bar on the reading that matches the evaluator's actual
  needs; needs a spec call to decide whether to chase the
  definitional gap.

## Taxonomy gaps surfaced

Three regulatory categories that turned up in the recall sample but
have no taxonomy entry to receive a tag:

| gap | example fragment | example text |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sign regulations | 9004, 8910 | "ground sign shall not exceed 2.0 square metres in area, and shall not exceed a height of 1.2 metres" |
| Landscaping requirements | 8669 | "the soft landscaping on any flat roof, as per the requirements of Section 418" |
| Building dimensions other than height/footprint | 6782 | "a building width of 38.0 metres" (no `building_width_m` in taxonomy) |

The taxonomy already has `secondary_use_classes`, `lot_coverage_percent`,
and `building_height_m` for the most common Halifax regulations.
Adding sign, landscaping, and width attributes would mostly help
discoverability for submissions that name those characteristics —
they don't affect today's evaluator coverage.

## Idempotency check

A re-run with the same `--model` + `--commit-every` against the same
taxonomy version should report `unchanged_skipped` close to the
previous `tagged + (1514 - tagged)` count and write zero new rows.
This was *not* exercised against prod (the first run cost is already
in; a re-run is not worth the spend until either the model or
taxonomy changes). The behaviour is covered by
`test_idempotent_rerun_is_noop` in `tests/test_enrich_attribute_tags.py`
and will be observed naturally the next time anyone reruns the script
after a `phase1-2026-05` no-op patch.

## Downstream smoke check

A plausible ER-2 Halifax submission (`zone_code=ER-2`,
`building_height_m=11`, setbacks 4.5/6.0/1.5/1.5, `lot_area_m2=500`,
`residential_unit_count=2`) was run through
`evaluate_submission_against_bylaws` against `document_id = 4`
(`scripts/abs71_smoke_check.py`). Result:

| attribute | verdict | delta | applicable_clauses |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `zone_code = ER-2` | `uncertain` | — | 8 |
| `building_height_m = 11.0` | `uncertain` | — | 8 |
| `front_setback_m = 4.5` | `uncertain` | required ≥ 2.0 m, submitted 4.5 m | 4 |
| `rear_setback_m = 6.0` | `uncertain` | — | 8 |
| `side_setback_left_m = 1.5` | `uncertain` | — | 8 |
| `side_setback_right_m = 1.5` | `uncertain` | — | 8 |
| `lot_area_m2 = 500.0` | **`fail`** | required ≥ 600 m², shortfall 100 m² | 8 |
| `residential_unit_count = 2` | `uncertain` | — | 8 |

`overall_status = non_compliant`, driven by the `lot_area_m2` fail.

The "done when" criterion ("at least one non-`uncertain` verdict")
is satisfied by `lot_area_m2 = fail`. More importantly, **every
attribute is now finding 4–8 applicable clauses via the tag filter** —
before enrichment, every attribute would have returned 0 clauses and
`uncertain` with no delta. The remaining `uncertain` verdicts trace
back to the regex constraint extractor not picking out a structured
operator/value from the relevant clauses, which is a Phase-2 problem
([ABS-42](https://linear.app/agenticbylawsystems/issue/ABS-42) parent
scope), not a tagging problem. `front_setback_m` shows the shape we
want everywhere: a delta is computed even when the verdict aggregator
hedges on conflicting clauses.

## Reproducing this report

```bash
# 1. Tunnel to prod Postgres via docker bridge
ssh -fN -L 5471:172.18.0.2:5432 bylaw-prod

# 2. Spot-check sampler (deterministic seed=71)
DATABASE_URL="postgresql+psycopg://layer1:<pwd>@localhost:5471/layer1" \
    .venv/bin/python scripts/abs71_spot_check_sample.py > sample.md

# 3. Smoke check
DATABASE_URL="postgresql+psycopg://layer1:<pwd>@localhost:5471/layer1" \
    .venv/bin/python scripts/abs71_smoke_check.py
```

Both scripts are checked in under `scripts/` and import only from
`src/layer1` / `src/layer2`; they read prod Postgres but never write
to it (idempotent reads).
